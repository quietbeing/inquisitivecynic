What I've been doing

For the past twelve years I've led fleet and business operations at Emory University. As Assistant Director, I direct strategic management of a 585-vehicle fleet across five Georgia locations — roughly $7M in assets on a $2M annual operating budget. The results I'm proudest of are the quiet, compounding kind: cutting total fleet cost-per-mile nearly in half, right-sizing the fleet from 664 vehicles to 585 to lower carrying costs, and deploying the information and telematics systems that made the whole operation legible for the first time. Before that, as Manager of Business Operations, I built the in-house strategic fleet program from research up and reported the financial performance of parking, transportation, and fleet to university leadership.

Earlier, running campus parking for Standard Parking, I managed daily operations across 14 facilities and 13,000 spaces with a team of 65, and led the work that more than doubled annual visitor parking revenue over two years — through pricing strategy, disciplined loss prevention, and rigorous daily financial control, not by adding a single space. It's where I learned that the revenue in an operation is usually hiding in plain sight, waiting for someone to ask the right question.

Before parking and fleet, I managed all the operational functions — payroll, budgeting, IT, HR — of a nonprofit, and ran a $1.75M corporate-accounts portfolio at Six Flags Over Georgia. Different industries, same throughline: take responsibility for how something runs, measure it honestly, and make it better.

Not the hype, the practice. It's the same discipline I've always brought to a telematics feed or a maintenance invoice, pointed at a new set of tools.
